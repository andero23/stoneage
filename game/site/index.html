A survival sim of the Mesolithic — c. 10,000 years ago

Stay or roam.

Lead your tribe through the Stone Age. Every spring and autumn a window opens and you choose: stay in the place you built, or leave it behind.

You decide how you play: keep the band unseen and let strangers pass, or be the ones who come in the night.

It runs on a phone, but the game reads better on a larger screen.

A winter count reckoned the years in winters — each one marked with a single image on hide. Your score is the same: it adds every season you survive, and it stops when the band dies out.

Every hut makes leaving harder

Stay, and the stores fill, the band grows, and so does everything you would lose — and how far away you can be seen. Move, and you stay small, poor, and unfound. Both roads end the same way. The only question is how long you last, and the game never tells you which choice was right.

Three things other games don't do

01

Relics have stories, not stats

An eagle's wing that Aro carried back from the north, the winter the river never froze. It changes something. You are never shown what.

02

Some rituals do nothing

Which ones is decided when the world is made, and never revealed. You will build your own superstition, and it will be yours.

03

People are named, and cannot be replaced

When the old forager dies, the knowledge of which mushrooms are safe dies with her. The poisonings start again.

All of this happened

Built on real archaeology: the antler headdresses of Star Carr, the trapezoid houses of Lepenski Vir, Göbekli Tepe — where hunter-gatherers raised a temple thousands of years before anyone farmed a field.
